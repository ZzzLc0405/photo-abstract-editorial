# Photo-Derived Abstract Prompt (English)

Treat the uploaded photograph strictly as the sole source of visual information. Create exactly one standalone abstract artwork and one variation only. The source photograph is for internal analysis and must not appear, be reproduced, or remain recognizable as a photographic region in the final canvas. Include no title, subtitle, caption, label, or other text.

## Method

Use this sequence internally without outputting analysis:

**DECONSTRUCT → SELECTIVE PRESERVATION → ABSTRACT / DISTILL → RECONSTRUCT**

1. Identify three to six decisive facts: subject masses, relative scale, position, axes, direction, intervals, repetition, overlap, depth, tonal hierarchy, color roles, and negative space.
2. Remove surface texture, perspective detail, background noise, and low-information ornament.
3. Reconstruct the retained relationships with the fewest possible marks.
4. Make the result read first as a restrained abstract composition and only on second glance evoke the particular source photograph.

This is not style transfer, vectorization, tracing, posterization, a filtered photo, or an illustration of the scene.

## Abstraction

- Ordinary scenes, landscapes, horizons, water, and crowds: retain direction, density, intervals, hierarchy, movement, and color relationships; discard complete object outlines.
- Distinctive architecture or subjects: retain at most one to three identity cues, such as an outer mass, negative space, eave line, taper, arch, spire, or layered rhythm; omit surface detail.
- Organic groups: express density and movement with overlapping soft organic masses; omit internal patterns and realistic highlights.
- People: use one continuous irregular short vertical mark or gently tapered block per person; never draw separate heads, limbs, faces, or clothing.
- Roads, railings, horizons, and shores: compress them into one or two fine axes with a few irregular interruptions.
- Small representative objects: retain only two or three planar marks and establish recognition through scale and position, not realistic material or volume.

Preserve minimum necessary recognizability rather than replicating objects.

## Mark and color system

Use one primary mark family and no more than two supporting families. Suitable families include flat or slightly organic color blocks, soft irregular masses, arcing or tapered strokes, short bars, layered bands, fine structural lines, and simplified architectural masses.

Every mark must correspond to a fact in the photograph. Avoid decorative additions, fabricated symmetry, regularized spacing, generic icons, and infographic geometry. Let scale, position, and spacing vary subtly with an observed natural rhythm.

Extract all colors from the photograph, reduce their number, and lower their saturation. Use one dominant role, one dark structural role, one light or neutral role, and at most one or two small accents that genuinely exist in the source.

## Canvas and layout

Create one standalone abstract canvas with no photographic area, split layout, diptych, panel join, frame, border, collage, tape, card, shadow, or mockup effect.

Use a completely flat, even, continuous neutral-ivory background, preferably **#F3F0E8** or a harmonious nearby ivory. Place the motif near the center, lower-middle, or at an asymmetrical position supported by the source relationships. Keep generous whitespace, normally about 55–75% of the canvas. Let horizontal subjects spread wider and vertical subjects rise taller, but do not fill the canvas or reduce the motif to a generic icon.

The canvas orientation may follow the source composition unless the user specifies another format.

## Clean mode and output

Do not use gradients, lighting variation, glow, shadows, vignettes, bands, seams, paper texture, grain, noise, fibers, watercolor underpainting, fog, stains, fading, scan marks, pasted textures, or compression artifacts. Slightly natural edges and handmade irregularity are allowed only within the abstract marks.

Use one image-generation call and request one result. Output, attach, and display exactly one finished text-free abstract artwork. Never provide multiple candidates, variants, duplicate images, or before/after images, and never place two copies within one canvas. If the tool unexpectedly returns duplicates, keep and return only the first unique image. Include no source photo, photographic fragment, title, subtitle, caption, letters, numbers, dates, serial marks, legends, signatures, logos, or watermarks.
